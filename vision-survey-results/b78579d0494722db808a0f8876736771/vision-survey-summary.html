Vision Weekend Survey · Results · September 2026

What the team said

25 of 35 participants responded, averaging 4.1 of 5 across the 25 rating questions. The vision statement and the core values draw the strongest agreement: 76–100%, with Worship and Discipleship the highest-rated items in the survey. The emphasis follows at 72–80%. The four objectives hold 68–88% support but 60–64% understanding, and are where low ratings concentrate. The lowest figure in the survey is confidence that the work will shape the decisions the church makes (64%); the highest end-of-survey figure is willingness to stay involved (21 of 25).

Observations

One reading of the data above, placed last so it frames nothing. Every question’s own distribution of answers is in the data document.

The vision and values are confirmed.

Support for the vision statement runs 22 of 25 at 4–5. Worship and Discipleship scored highest in the survey. Agape belongs (84%) but is less understood (76%).

Agreement is lower on the objectives than on the vision and values.

Vision and values 76–100%. Emphasis 72–80%. Objectives 68–88% support but 60–64% understanding. “Will shape the decisions our church makes” sits at 64%, the survey’s lowest. On the emphasis the team is surer of itself than of each other: ten strongly agree it reflects their own view, four that it reflects the group’s.

Understanding of the objectives is the survey’s weakest area.

9–10 of 25 score low on understanding for every one of the four. The six lowest scorers account for 81 of 131 low ratings (62%), but the gap extends past them: 4–5 of the other 19 rate their understanding of each objective low. Support thins across the year, Assessment (84%) to Efficiency (68%). Counted per person the team splits rather than shading gradually: 11 are clear on all four objectives and 9 on one or none, with almost no one in between.

Confidence is lower than willingness to stay involved.

16 agree, 7 neutral and 2 disagree that it will shape decisions: mostly unsure rather than opposed. 21 of 25 would gladly stay involved.

The framing is held more widely than its origin is remembered.

21 of 25 agree the ministry-review framing matches their understanding; six cannot trace how the team arrived at it. The Genysys report went out with the invitation, so agreement here partly tracks who read it that week.

The appetite to meet again cannot be read as unanimous.

Both answers offered were ways to meet, so the question cannot measure opposition to meeting. Against the whole invited team, the group asking to meet whatever the results show is 16 of 35 (46%).

About this report. Prepared by the survey facilitator with AI assistance. Reason-checklist questions were answered almost entirely by low scorers as instructed (58 of 60 answers) and are summarized as sentiment, not counts. Every closed-ended question, in the survey’s own wording and order, is in the data document.

Caveats. One person is 4 points, so read patterns rather than single differences. The 10 non-respondents are unmeasured and excluded from every figure but the response rate. Percent agreeing (4–5) is sturdier than the means shown in hovers. All items were positively worded, so comparisons between pieces are more reliable than any single level. The reconvene question offered no way to decline.
